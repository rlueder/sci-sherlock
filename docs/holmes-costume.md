# Holmes: costume and pipe brief

Applies to every new standing, walking and gesture pose. The scene is London in
November 1895 (`teaser.md`). Holmes's age of 58–62 is deliberate art direction.
Keep the approved v6 scale: approximately 106 native pixels tall, natural adult
proportions, the existing 64-colour palette and three-quarter camera.

## Evidence and interpretation

| Element | Evidence | Production decision |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe | Doyle explicitly gives Holmes a black clay pipe in *The Red-Headed League* and *A Case of Identity*. In *The Copper Beeches*, cherry-wood replaces clay when he is disputatious rather than meditative. [Original 1892 collection](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/48320/48320-h/48320-h.htm) | Workshop/thinking default: black clay, a slender near-straight dark stem and modest upright bowl. Material and colour are textual; exact geometry is our interpretation. Do not describe this as his only canonical pipe. |
| Suit layers | [J. B. Johnstone's British wool suit, 1894, Met 2009.300.548a–c](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/159090) documents coat, waistcoat and trousers; its morning-coat cut is for daytime/formal wear. [LACMA's 1885–1895 suit](https://collections.lacma.org/object/4243) is an American comparative reference, not proof of a particular London outfit. | Use period layers and natural tailoring; do not copy a morning cutaway as an overcoat. Our scene costume is an interpretation, not an outfit specified verbatim by Doyle. |
| Outerwear | November weather and the approved long-coat silhouette establish the need for an overcoat. | Plain dark wool, knee length, modest lapels, single-breasted construction, natural shoulders, functional skirt overlap. Keep room for the clothing beneath it. No trench hardware, zip or exaggerated modern fashion cut. |
| Shirt and trousers | Late-Victorian tailoring brief, interpreted at sprite scale. | White stiff high collar, small dark tie; high-waisted straight wool trousers with ease, plain black low-heeled ankle boots. Oxblood waistcoat preserves the approved colour direction. |
| Watch | Requested character business; a period pocket watch, not a wristwatch. | Small single Albert-style chain attached to waistcoat buttonhole and pocket, not outer coat. Show that same attachment during retrieval and return. Hardware and chain colour are design choices. |
| Hat | User explicitly retains the iconic deerstalker. Doyle describes a close-fitting cloth cap in *The Boscombe Valley Mystery* in the collection above. | Brown tweed double-peaked cap with tied earflaps. Retention indoors/in town is a deliberate character signature, not a claim about every canonical appearance. |

The pipe must not become a large curved calabash or a pale oversized bowl. At game
scale its dark stem should read as a small connected cluster; do not enlarge the
head or figure to display it. The chain may be occluded by coat or arm. Do not invent
extra bright detail to make every accessory visible in every pose.

## Full-figure workflow

1. Render/draw the entire pose with coherent shoulders, elbows, wrists, torso and
   clothing. The rejected r5 limb-grafting method must not be reused.
2. Inspect the costume, age and anatomy before making animation frames.
3. Draw full-figure contact/down/passing/up poses and gesture transitions. Preserve
   camera, limb lengths, costume construction and cloth overlap throughout.
4. Convert complete figures to the shared palette with binary alpha and nearest
   sampling. Keep one scale per sheet; padding changes must not change body scale.
5. Review native pixels in the room, then motion and planted-foot continuity. A valid
   PNG or successful export is not an anatomy or animation approval.

The [r6 study](../art/studies/holmes-r6/README.md) contains the first costume revision.
Its key poses are review material, not a completed walk cycle or engine delivery.
